What TrueJoule found

  • Unseen bearings change the answer. Holding out whole bearings reverses the model ranking (Kendall τ −0.21 on CWRU) and leaves 2 of 9 Pareto-optimal models standing. The CNN that wins on random splits costs 44–4,729× the cheapest tree; on unseen bearings a forest (CWRU) or an envelope-feature MLP (Paderborn) is best.
  • The front end is the cost. 99.8 % of a decision tree's energy is feature extraction; a float-only logarithm made the log-spectrum front end 3.17× cheaper.
  • Estimates are rankings, not budgets. Chip-level estimates miss the measured energy by 0.31–5.35× and move models on and off the Pareto front.
  • Verify before you measure. The board check exposed an int8 kernel fault in the Arduino TFLM port that left energy unchanged and predictions wrong; after the fix 50 of 50 TFLM models are bit-exact.
  • Calibrate on the machine. A gate that re-fits itself from 1.4 s of healthy vibration cut false alarms at an unseen load from 1.00 to 0.023.
